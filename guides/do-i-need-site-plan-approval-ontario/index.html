Do I Need Site Plan Approval in Ontario?

Published August 2026· Updated August 2026· 6 min read· PreBuildIQ Research Team
Quick Answer

You need site plan approval in Ontario when your development falls within an area the municipality has designated as a site plan control area and is not exempt, which covers most commercial, industrial, institutional, and multi-residential development and additions. Detached and semi-detached houses are commonly exempt, though some municipalities and recent provincial changes have adjusted small-residential thresholds. Site plan approval controls how a site is developed (access, servicing, grading, landscaping, massing); it is separate from and precedes the building permit.

Site plan control is the approval developers most often underestimate, because it sounds administrative. It is not. It governs access, grading, servicing, landscaping, and building placement, and it must be secured before a building permit issues. Assuming a project is exempt when it is not is a fast way to have a building permit application stall at intake.

What Triggers Site Plan Control

Site plan control applies where two things are true: the land is within a site plan control area designated by the municipality, and the proposed development is not exempted. Most municipalities designate broad areas, so the real question is usually whether your specific development type is caught. Common triggers include:

  • New commercial, industrial, or institutional buildings
  • Multi-unit residential (apartments, stacked and street townhouses)
  • Additions and expansions to existing non-residential buildings
  • Mixed-use development
  • Significant changes to parking, access, or grading on a site

What Is Usually Exempt

Detached houses, semi-detached houses, and very small residential projects are commonly exempt from site plan control, though this is exactly the area provincial reforms have been adjusting to speed up housing. Some municipalities exempt minor additions below a floor-area threshold. Because exemptions are set locally and have been changing, the safe move is to confirm the current threshold for your municipality rather than rely on a general rule.

Site Plan Approval vs Building Permit

They are different approvals with different purposes, and site plan comes first. Site plan control governs how the site works: access points, grading and drainage, servicing connections, landscaping, and the placement and exterior of the building. The building permit governs whether the building itself complies with the Ontario Building Code. A site plan agreement is typically registered on title, and its conditions become preconditions to issuing the building permit.

What Site Plan Approval Requires

A site plan submission is drawing-heavy and study-supported. Expect a site plan, grading and servicing plans, a landscape plan, and elevations, plus supporting studies that vary by site: a Functional Servicing Report, a Stormwater Management Report, a Traffic Impact Study, an arborist report, and often an Urban Design Brief. The exact list is confirmed at pre-application consultation and is where budgets are set.

Example
A developer treated a modest commercial addition as a building-permit-only project. The municipality confirmed the site was in a site plan control area and the addition exceeded the local exemption threshold, so a site plan application, a servicing report, and a landscape plan were required first. The building permit could not be issued until the site plan agreement was signed, adding several months.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do I need site plan approval in Ontario?

You need site plan approval if your property is within a municipally designated site plan control area and your development is not exempt. This covers most commercial, industrial, institutional, multi-residential, and mixed-use development and additions. Detached and semi-detached houses are commonly exempt, though thresholds have been changing under provincial reform.

What triggers site plan control?

Site plan control is triggered when land is in a designated site plan control area and the development is a non-exempt type: new commercial, industrial, institutional, or multi-unit residential buildings, additions to non-residential buildings, mixed-use projects, and significant changes to parking, access, or grading.

What is the difference between site plan approval and a building permit?

Site plan approval governs how the site is developed, its access, grading, servicing, landscaping, and building placement, under the Planning Act. A building permit governs whether the building complies with the Ontario Building Code. Site plan approval comes first, and its conditions are usually preconditions to issuing the building permit.

Is a single detached house exempt from site plan approval?

Usually yes. Detached and semi-detached houses are commonly exempt from site plan control, and provincial reforms have pushed to keep small-residential development out of site plan approval to speed up housing. Because exemption thresholds are set locally and have changed, confirm the current rule for your municipality.

What studies are required for site plan approval?

Site plan approval typically requires a site plan, grading and servicing plans, a landscape plan, and elevations, plus supporting studies that vary by site: a Functional Servicing Report, a Stormwater Management Report, a Traffic Impact Study, an arborist report, and often an Urban Design Brief. The exact list is confirmed at pre-application consultation.
