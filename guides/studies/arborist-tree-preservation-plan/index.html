What Is an Arborist Report and Tree Preservation Plan in Ontario?

Published August 2026· Updated August 2026· 7 min read· PreBuildIQ Research Team
Quick Answer

An Arborist Report and Tree Preservation Plan inventory the trees on and near a site, assess their health, and set out which trees are protected, which can be removed, and what compensation or replanting is required. They are triggered by most site plan, subdivision, and rezoning applications and by municipal tree by-laws that protect trees above a certain size. They are prepared by a certified arborist and often determine building placement, because a protected tree’s root zone can be a no-build area.

A single protected tree can move a building. Municipal tree by-laws and site plan conditions give mature trees a protected root zone that construction cannot enter, so the arborist report is not a formality, it is a constraint map. Developers who site the building first and inventory the trees second routinely find a tree exactly where the foundation was meant to go.

What Is a Arborist and Tree Preservation Plan?

An Arborist Report is a professional assessment of the trees on and adjacent to a site: species, size, health, and condition. The Tree Preservation Plan that accompanies it maps which trees are retained, which are removed, and the protection measures, fencing, root zones, for those that stay. Together they establish tree-related conditions of approval.

Most Ontario municipalities have a tree protection or private tree by-law that requires a permit to injure or remove trees above a threshold diameter. The arborist report supports that permit and quantifies the compensation, replacement plantings or cash-in-lieu, owed for approved removals.

When Is It Required?

An Arborist Report and Tree Preservation Plan are triggered by:

  • Site plan control and subdivision applications with existing trees
  • Any removal of trees protected under a municipal tree by-law
  • Rezoning of treed or partially treed sites
  • Development adjacent to a woodland, ravine, or city-owned trees
  • Sites where trees straddle or overhang a property line

Who Prepares It

An Arborist Report is prepared by a certified arborist, commonly an International Society of Arboriculture (ISA) Certified Arborist, and for larger or ecologically sensitive sites by a consulting arborist or forester. Municipal urban forestry staff review the report and issue the associated tree permit.

What It Must Contain

A complete Arborist Report and Tree Preservation Plan typically contain:

  • A tree inventory with species, diameter, and health condition
  • Identification of protected and boundary trees
  • Trees to be retained, removed, and the rationale
  • Tree protection zones, fencing, and construction measures
  • Replacement planting or cash-in-lieu compensation
  • An arborist supervision and monitoring plan during construction

Budget note

Compensation for removed trees, whether replanting or cash-in-lieu, is a separate cost from the report itself, and in cities with aggressive canopy targets it can run to tens of thousands of dollars. Price the compensation, not just the study.

How Requirements Vary Across Ontario Municipalities

Tree by-laws differ sharply by municipality. Toronto protects private trees above 30 cm diameter and city trees of any size, with significant compensation. Many suburban municipalities protect trees above a set diameter or within defined areas only. Vaughan, Oakville, Mississauga, and others each set their own thresholds and replacement ratios, so the same tree removal can be free in one municipality and cost thousands in the next.

Example
A small infill project planned to remove three mature trees at the rear of a lot. The arborist report found one was a boundary tree shared with the neighbour, requiring their consent, and the city required three replacement trees for each removal. The permit and compensation reshaped both the rear grading and the landscape budget.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is an arborist report for development?

An arborist report is a certified professional’s assessment of the trees on and near a site, their species, size, health, and condition, paired with a Tree Preservation Plan showing which trees are retained, which are removed, and the protection measures. It supports the tree removal permit and sets the compensation owed for approved removals.

When do I need a tree preservation plan in Ontario?

You need one for most site plan, subdivision, and rezoning applications on treed sites, and whenever you propose to remove trees protected under a municipal tree by-law. Development adjacent to woodlands, ravines, or city-owned trees, and sites with boundary trees, also trigger the requirement.

How much does an arborist report cost in Ontario?

An arborist report typically costs between $2,000 and $12,000 or more, driven by the number of trees, the presence of boundary or city trees, and whether construction-phase monitoring is required. Compensation for removed trees, replacement planting or cash-in-lieu, is a separate and sometimes larger cost.

Do I need a permit to remove a tree for development?

Usually yes. Most Ontario municipalities have a tree protection or private tree by-law requiring a permit to remove or injure trees above a threshold diameter, and city-owned trees are protected regardless of size. The arborist report supports the permit application and quantifies replacement or cash-in-lieu compensation.

Who prepares a tree preservation plan?

A tree preservation plan and arborist report are prepared by a certified arborist, commonly an ISA Certified Arborist, or a consulting arborist or forester for larger sites. Municipal urban forestry staff review the report and issue the associated tree permit.
