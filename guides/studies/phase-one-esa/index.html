What Is a Phase I Environmental Site Assessment (ESA) in Ontario?

Published August 2026· Updated August 2026· 7 min read· PreBuildIQ Research Team
Quick Answer

A Phase I Environmental Site Assessment (ESA) is a due-diligence study that reviews a property’s history and current condition to identify potential contamination from past uses. It is required when land use changes to a more sensitive use (for example industrial to residential), which under Ontario’s Record of Site Condition regime can compel a filed RSC. It is prepared by a qualified person, involves no sampling at Phase I, and a finding of potential contamination triggers a Phase II ESA with actual soil and groundwater testing.

The Phase I ESA is the gate to the most expensive surprise in land development: contamination. It is a records-and-inspection study with no drilling, but its conclusion decides everything downstream. A clean Phase I lets the project proceed; a flagged one opens a Phase II, and potentially remediation and a Record of Site Condition that can add years and millions.

What Is a Phase I Environmental Site Assessment?

A Phase I Environmental Site Assessment is a non-intrusive due-diligence study that evaluates whether a property is likely to be contaminated. It combines a review of historical records, aerial photographs, and title, a site reconnaissance, and interviews to identify areas of potential environmental concern from past and present land uses. No soil or groundwater sampling is done at this stage.

In Ontario the Phase I ESA is tied to the Record of Site Condition (RSC) regime under Ontario Regulation 153/04. When land changes to a more sensitive use, such as commercial or industrial to residential or parkland, an RSC is generally required to be filed, and that process begins with a Phase I ESA prepared by a qualified person. If the Phase I identifies potential contamination, a Phase II ESA with intrusive sampling follows.

When Is It Required?

A Phase I ESA is triggered by change of use and by lender or diligence needs, including:

  • Change to a more sensitive use (industrial or commercial to residential)
  • Any development requiring a filed Record of Site Condition
  • Sites with a history of industrial, automotive, or fuel-related use
  • Lender or purchaser due diligence on a land acquisition
  • Brownfield redevelopment and municipal incentive programs

Who Prepares It

A Phase I ESA must be prepared by a qualified person (QP) as defined in Ontario Regulation 153/04, typically an environmental professional who is a Professional Engineer or Professional Geoscientist meeting the regulation’s criteria. The same QP oversees any subsequent Phase II ESA and the Record of Site Condition submission.

What It Must Contain

A complete Phase I ESA typically contains:

  • A records review: historical land use, aerial photos, title, and directories
  • A site reconnaissance for evidence of contamination
  • Interviews with owners, occupants, and municipal sources
  • Identification of areas of potential environmental concern
  • A conclusion on whether a Phase II ESA is needed
  • Documentation meeting Ontario Regulation 153/04 where an RSC is required

Budget note

The Phase I fee is small, but it is a gate, not an endpoint. If it triggers a Phase II, remediation, and a Record of Site Condition, the cost and timeline escalate dramatically. On any former industrial or commercial site, budget for the possibility of the full RSC path, not just the Phase I.

How Requirements Vary Across Ontario Municipalities

The Record of Site Condition requirement is provincial, set by Ontario Regulation 153/04, so the trigger, change to a more sensitive use, is consistent statewide. What varies is municipal practice: many municipalities will not issue planning approvals or building permits for a sensitive use until the RSC is filed on the Environmental Site Registry, and brownfield-focused municipalities offer Community Improvement Plan incentives that require the ESA process. Former employment lands converting to residential across the GTA almost always run this path.

Example
A developer bought a former automotive-service site to build townhouses. The Phase I ESA flagged potential contamination from historic fuel storage, triggering a Phase II that confirmed impacted soil. Remediation and filing a Record of Site Condition added over a year to the schedule before the residential use could be approved, a risk the Phase I surfaced at the diligence stage.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is a Phase I Environmental Site Assessment?

A Phase I Environmental Site Assessment (ESA) is a non-intrusive due-diligence study that reviews a property’s history and current condition to identify potential contamination. It uses records, a site inspection, and interviews, with no soil sampling. If it identifies potential contamination, a Phase II ESA with actual testing follows. It is prepared by a qualified person.

When is a Phase I ESA required in Ontario?

A Phase I ESA is required when land use changes to a more sensitive use, such as industrial or commercial to residential, which under Ontario Regulation 153/04 generally requires a filed Record of Site Condition. It is also standard for lender and purchaser due diligence and for sites with an industrial or fuel-related history.

How much does a Phase I ESA cost?

A Phase I ESA typically costs between $3,000 and $10,000 or more, driven by site size, the complexity of the property’s history, and whether documentation must meet Record of Site Condition standards. The larger costs come only if the Phase I triggers a Phase II, remediation, and an RSC.

What is a Record of Site Condition?

A Record of Site Condition (RSC) is a filing on Ontario’s Environmental Site Registry certifying that a property meets the applicable site condition standards for its intended use. It is generally required when land changes to a more sensitive use, and the process begins with a Phase I ESA. Many municipalities will not approve a sensitive use until the RSC is filed.

What is the difference between a Phase I and a Phase II ESA?

A Phase I ESA is a non-intrusive records and inspection study that identifies whether contamination is likely. A Phase II ESA is the intrusive follow-up, drilling boreholes and sampling soil and groundwater, done only if the Phase I identifies potential concerns. A clean Phase I means no Phase II is needed.
