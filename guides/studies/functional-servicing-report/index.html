What Is a Functional Servicing Report (FSR) in Ontario?

Published August 2026· Updated August 2026· 7 min read· PreBuildIQ Research Team
Quick Answer

A Functional Servicing Report (FSR) demonstrates that a proposed development can be serviced with municipal water, sanitary sewer, and stormwater management within available capacity. It is required for almost all zoning by-law amendments, subdivisions, and site plan applications on serviced land. It is prepared by a civil engineer, is very often combined with the Stormwater Management Report, and is the study that most commonly reveals a capacity constraint that can stall or resize a project.

The Functional Servicing Report is where a project meets physics. Zoning can permit a tower, but if the local sanitary sewer has no capacity, the FSR is the document that says so, and it says so early enough to change the project before the developer has spent on architecture. Municipalities return more applications for a deficient FSR than for almost any other study.

What Is a Functional Servicing Report?

A Functional Servicing Report is a civil engineering study that confirms a site can be connected to and served by municipal water supply, sanitary sewer, and stormwater infrastructure. It sizes the required connections, checks them against available municipal capacity, and identifies any upgrades the development must fund.

In practice the FSR is usually delivered as a combined Functional Servicing and Stormwater Management Report, because the two are analytically linked: how a site drains determines what stormwater controls it needs, which affects grading, servicing, and site layout. The report is preliminary or functional at the application stage and is refined into detailed design later.

When Is It Required?

A Functional Servicing Report is required for essentially any intensification or new development on municipally serviced land, including:

  • Zoning by-law amendment and official plan amendment applications
  • Plans of subdivision and condominium
  • Site plan control applications for new buildings or additions
  • Any proposal that increases water demand or sanitary flow
  • Sites where downstream sewer or watermain capacity is known to be constrained

Who Prepares It

A Functional Servicing Report is prepared by a civil engineer licensed as a Professional Engineer (P.Eng.) in Ontario, typically within a land development engineering firm. The same firm usually prepares the Stormwater Management Report and the grading and servicing drawings, keeping the analysis consistent.

What It Must Contain

A complete Functional Servicing Report typically contains:

  • Water demand and available supply / pressure analysis
  • Sanitary sewage generation and downstream capacity assessment
  • Stormwater management approach and quantity / quality controls
  • Preliminary grading and servicing plan
  • Identification of required municipal infrastructure upgrades
  • Confirmation of conformance with municipal design criteria

Budget note

If the FSR reveals a downstream capacity constraint, the developer may have to fund an off-site infrastructure upgrade or accept a reduced unit count. This is the study most likely to change project economics, so scope it before finalizing density.

How Requirements Vary Across Ontario Municipalities

Every serviced municipality requires an FSR, but each publishes its own design criteria for water, wastewater, and stormwater, and two-tier regions add a regional servicing standard. Credit Valley Conservation, the Toronto and Region Conservation Authority, and other conservation authorities also review the stormwater components where a regulated feature is nearby. The combined FSR/SWM report therefore often satisfies three reviewers at once, each of which can return it.

Example
An intensification proposal on a fully serviced lot assumed the existing sanitary connection could carry the added units. The Functional Servicing Report found the downstream sewer was already near capacity, requiring either an off-site upgrade or a reduction of eight units. The developer chose the smaller building, a decision the FSR surfaced before any architectural spend.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is a Functional Servicing Report?

A Functional Servicing Report (FSR) is a civil engineering study that demonstrates a proposed development can be serviced with municipal water, sanitary sewer, and stormwater management within available capacity. It sizes the connections, checks them against municipal capacity, and identifies any infrastructure upgrades the development must fund. It is usually combined with the Stormwater Management Report.

When is a Functional Servicing Report required?

A Functional Servicing Report is required for almost any development or intensification on municipally serviced land: zoning by-law amendments, official plan amendments, subdivisions, condominiums, and site plan applications. Any proposal that increases water demand or sanitary flow, or sits on a capacity-constrained system, will trigger one.

How much does a Functional Servicing Report cost?

A Functional Servicing Report typically costs between $8,000 and $35,000 or more, driven by site size, stormwater complexity, and whether external capacity studies are needed. It is frequently delivered together with the Stormwater Management Report as a single combined report.

What is the difference between a Functional Servicing Report and a Stormwater Management Report?

The Functional Servicing Report covers water supply, sanitary sewer, and overall servicing; the Stormwater Management Report covers how the site handles rain and runoff. They are analytically linked and are usually combined into one Functional Servicing and Stormwater Management Report prepared by the same civil engineer.

Who prepares a Functional Servicing Report?

A Functional Servicing Report is prepared by a civil engineer licensed as a Professional Engineer (P.Eng.) in Ontario, typically within a land development engineering firm that also prepares the grading, servicing, and stormwater drawings.
