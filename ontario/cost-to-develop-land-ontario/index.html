What Does It Cost to Develop Land in Ontario? A 2026 Breakdown

Published August 2026· Updated August 2026· 8 min read· PreBuildIQ Research Team
Quick Answer

The cost of developing land in Ontario before construction falls into four buckets: municipal application fees, development charges, supporting studies and consultants, and carrying costs. Application fees for a zoning by-law amendment reach into the tens of thousands in larger municipalities (Mississauga’s base ZBA fee is over $37,000). Development charges add tens of thousands per unit. Supporting studies and consultants commonly total $50,000 to $250,000 for a mid-sized project. Carrying costs run about $43,000 per month while approvals are pending.

No Ontario government source publishes the total cost of developing land in one place, because the cost is spread across a city, a region, the province, and a roster of private consultants. This page assembles the four buckets so a developer can build a realistic pre-development budget instead of discovering each cost one deficiency notice at a time.

Bucket 1: Planning Application Fees

Municipalities charge a fee to process each planning application, and for major applications those fees are substantial. A zoning by-law amendment in a large GTA municipality runs into the tens of thousands: Mississauga’s base ZBA fee exceeds $37,000, and combined official plan and zoning amendments cost more. Site plan and subdivision fees are similarly scaled to unit count or floor area. Minor variances, by contrast, are modest, often in the low thousands or less. Fees are adjusted annually, typically by a cost-of-living factor, so confirm the current schedule.

Watch the variable fees

Major-application fees in many municipalities are not flat. They add a per-unit or per-square-metre charge on top of a base fee, so a large project’s application fee can be several multiples of the base. Read the fee worksheet, not just the headline number.

Bucket 2: Development Charges

Development charges (DCs) fund the municipal and regional infrastructure a new development draws on, and they are among the largest pre-construction costs, typically tens of thousands of dollars per residential unit in the GTA. Rates vary widely by municipality and are set out in each municipality’s DC by-law. Recent federal-provincial programs have reduced some headline rates, and Bill 17 allows many residential DCs to be deferred to occupancy rather than paid at building permit, which lowers the financing cost even where the amount is unchanged.

Because DCs are so large and so local, they deserve a per-city look. PreBuildIQ maintains development charge pages for major GTA municipalities.

Bucket 3: Studies and Consultants

The supporting studies a development application requires are the most variable cost, because the list depends entirely on what the site triggers. A simple site may need three or four studies; a complex one near a natural feature or a heritage property may need a dozen. Typical individual study ranges:

Add these together and a mid-sized project routinely spends $50,000 to $250,000 on studies and consultants before an application is complete. The single largest lever on this bucket is not negotiating rates; it is not commissioning studies the site does not actually trigger, and not re-commissioning studies because the first submission was incomplete.

Bucket 4: Carrying Cost

Every month a project sits in the approval queue, it carries the cost of the land, financing, and property taxes with no offsetting revenue. On a typical stalled GTA development that carrying cost is about $43,000 per month. Because approvals routinely take one to two years, and longer for rezonings, carrying cost is often the largest single line in the pre-development budget, and the one most directly worsened by a delay. A single incomplete first submission that adds three to four months can cost more than the entire study budget.

Example
A mid-rise developer budgeted application fees and studies but not the carrying cost of a two-year rezoning. Two study revisions and a regional co-approval extended the timeline by eight months beyond plan. At roughly $43,000 per month, the overrun alone exceeded $340,000, more than the project’s entire consultant budget.

How to Control the Cost

The buckets a developer can most influence are studies and carrying cost, and both are controlled at the same point: the completeness and accuracy of the first submission. Commission only the studies the site genuinely triggers, get them right the first time, and file a complete application so the statutory clock starts and does not reset. Everything else, fees and development charges, is largely fixed by the municipality; the leverage is in not paying for delay and duplication.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much does it cost to develop land in Ontario?

Pre-development cost in Ontario falls into four buckets: planning application fees (hundreds to over $40,000 per application), development charges (tens of thousands per unit), studies and consultants ($50,000 to $250,000+ for a mid-sized project), and carrying costs (about $43,000 per month while approvals are pending). The total depends heavily on the site and application type.

How much does a zoning by-law amendment cost in Ontario?

A zoning by-law amendment application fee in a large GTA municipality runs into the tens of thousands of dollars; Mississauga’s base ZBA fee exceeds $37,000, and combined official plan and zoning amendments cost more. Major-application fees often add per-unit or per-square-metre charges on top of the base. Fees are set by each municipality and adjusted annually.

How much do development studies cost in Ontario?

Individual studies range widely: a Planning Justification Report $8,000 to $40,000+, a Traffic Impact Study $6,000 to $30,000+, a Functional Servicing Report $8,000 to $35,000+, and an Environmental Impact Study $10,000 to $50,000+. A mid-sized project commonly spends $50,000 to $250,000 total, depending on how many studies the site triggers.

What are development charges in Ontario?

Development charges are fees municipalities and regions levy to fund the infrastructure a new development uses, typically tens of thousands of dollars per residential unit in the GTA. Rates are set in each municipality’s development charge by-law. Bill 17 allows many residential development charges to be paid at occupancy rather than at building permit.

What is the biggest cost in developing land in Ontario?

For most projects the largest pre-construction cost is carrying cost, the land, financing, and taxes paid while approvals are pending, at roughly $43,000 per month on a typical GTA project. Because approvals take one to two years or more, carrying cost often exceeds the combined total of fees and studies, and it is the cost most worsened by delay.
