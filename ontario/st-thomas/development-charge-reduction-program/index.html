St. Thomas Development Charge Reduction 2026: $19.6M Program Explained

Published October 2026· Updated October 2026· 6 min read· PreBuildIQ Research Team
Quick Answer

On October 15, 2026, the Governments of Canada and Ontario announced up to $19.6 million for the City of St. Thomas through the Development Charge Reduction Program, part of the Canada-Ontario Partnership to Build. In return, St. Thomas is reducing residential development charges by 30 percent for single-detached homes and multi-unit housing, and by 50 percent for all remaining housing types, for the period March 30, 2026 to March 31, 2029. The funding backfills the DC revenue St. Thomas forgoes by cutting the charge so the city can still fund growth infrastructure. The announcement follows similar federal-provincial DC reduction deals for Toronto, Mississauga, Peel Region, Vaughan, and other Ontario municipalities throughout 2025 and 2026.

St. Thomas has become the latest Ontario city to convert a federal-provincial funding commitment into a hard cut to development charges. The St. Thomas Context

St. Thomas, the county seat of Elgin County, has been one of the fastest-growing mid-sized cities in southwestern Ontario. The Amazon fulfilment centre and the Volkswagen EV battery plant at the nearby St. Thomas site have accelerated population projections significantly, creating demand for workforce and market housing at a scale the city was not previously planning for.

That growth context makes the DC reduction timing material: a city that needs to build fast and is facing a wave of demand from industrial employment gains benefits from anything that improves the economics of residential construction. For builders who had been watching the St. Thomas market, the 30 to 50 percent reduction to March 2029 changes the feasibility of projects that were marginal at full charge rates.

City portion only

The Development Charge Reduction Program applies to City of St. Thomas development charges. Elgin County development charges and education development charges are separate and are not the subject of this announcement. Pull the full charge stack before relying on any cost figure.

A small builder with land in St. Thomas had been waiting to see whether the market justified a 40-unit mixed residential project. At full development charge rates the project was marginal. The 50 percent reduction on the non-single-detached unit types in the mix, which included rental and affordable units, shifted the feasibility calculation. How This Fits the Ontario DC Reduction Wave

St. Thomas joins a growing list of Ontario municipalities that have entered DC reduction agreements under the Canada-Ontario Partnership to Build: Toronto (40 to 60 percent), Mississauga (50 percent, rental to zero), Peel Region (50 percent to November 2026), Vaughan (to zero for qualifying projects to October 2027), and Essex County (all types, to December 2030). The pattern is consistent: federal-provincial funding backfills the DC reserve, and the municipality agrees to hold a reduction for a defined window. For builders, the question is always the same: can the permit issue inside the window?

Frequently Asked Questions

How much are development charges being cut in St. Thomas in 2026?

St. Thomas is reducing development charges by 30 percent for single-detached homes and multi-unit housing, and by 50 percent for all remaining housing types, for the period March 30, 2026 to March 31, 2029, as part of an up-to-$19.6 million federal-provincial funding commitment announced October 15, 2026.

What is the $19.6 million for St. Thomas?

The up-to-$19.6 million comes from the Governments of Canada and Ontario through the Development Charge Reduction Program, part of the Canada-Ontario Partnership to Build. It backfills the development charge revenue St. Thomas forgoes by cutting the charge so the city can still fund growth infrastructure such as water, wastewater, and roads.

Does the St. Thomas DC cut apply to Elgin County charges?

No. The reduction applies to City of St. Thomas development charges only. Elgin County development charges and education development charges are separate and are not the subject of this announcement.

When does the St. Thomas development charge reduction end?

The reduction window runs from March 30, 2026 to March 31, 2029. Development charges are typically payable at building permit, so the practical deadline is getting a building permit within that window.

Why is St. Thomas growing so fast?

St. Thomas has benefited from major industrial investment, including an Amazon fulfilment centre and the Volkswagen EV battery plant nearby, which have accelerated population and housing demand projections significantly above prior municipal planning targets.
