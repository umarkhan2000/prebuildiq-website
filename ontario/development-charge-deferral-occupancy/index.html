Development Charge Deferral to Occupancy in Ontario: How Bill 17 Changes the Timing

Published August 2026· Updated August 2026· 6 min read· PreBuildIQ Research Team
Quick Answer

Under Bill 17, the Protect Ontario by Building Faster and Smarter Act, 2025, development charges for many residential projects in Ontario can be paid at occupancy rather than at building permit issuance. Building Code amendments enable occupancy permits for residential developments with deferred development charges, so a project can be occupied while the charge is paid on the deferred schedule. The deferral does not reduce the dollar amount of the charge; it moves the payment to the end of construction, materially lowering the financing cost of carrying that charge on a multi-year build.

Development charges have traditionally been due at building permit, the point where a project has spent heavily on approvals and design but generated no revenue. Carrying a five- or six-figure-per-unit charge through the entire construction period is pure financing cost. Bill 17 attacks that timing directly by letting the charge be paid at occupancy instead, when units are ready to generate income.

How the Deferral Works

Bill 17 amends the Development Charges Act to allow development charges for many residential projects to be paid at occupancy rather than at building permit issuance. To make that workable, the province paired it with Building Code amendments enabling occupancy permits for residential developments with deferred development charges. That lets a building be legally occupied while the deferred charge is paid on schedule, rather than holding occupancy hostage to full DC payment upfront.

What It Saves: A Cash-Flow Change, Not a Discount

The deferral does not lower the charge. The same amount is owed; it is simply owed later. The saving is the financing cost of not carrying that capital through construction. On a project where development charges run into the tens of thousands per unit and construction spans two or more years, deferring the charge to occupancy can remove a meaningful slice of carrying cost, which is why the change matters most on large, multi-year residential builds.

Because the amount is unchanged, the deferral stacks with the separate 2026 development charge reductions. A developer can both reduce the amount owed, where the project qualifies as affordable or rental or benefits from the funded reduction, and defer the remaining balance to occupancy. The two levers are complementary.

What to Confirm Before Relying on It

The deferral is enabled by Bill 17 but operationalized through regulation and municipal implementation. Before building it into a schedule, a developer should confirm three things:

  • That the project type is eligible for the occupancy-timing deferral.
  • That the enabling regulation and Building Code occupancy-permit provisions are in force.
  • That the municipality has implemented the deferral in its own development charge by-law and permit process.
Example
A developer of a two-tower residential project modelled development charges at building permit under the old timing and found the carrying cost significant. Applying the Bill 17 occupancy deferral, the charge moved to the end of construction, and an occupancy permit allowed the first tower to be occupied on schedule. The change reduced the project's financing cost without altering the total charge owed.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can development charges be deferred to occupancy in Ontario?

Yes. Under Bill 17, development charges for many residential projects in Ontario can be paid at occupancy rather than at building permit issuance. Building Code amendments enable occupancy permits for residential developments with deferred development charges, so a building can be occupied while the charge is paid on the deferred schedule.

Does deferring development charges reduce the amount owed?

No. The deferral changes when the charge is paid, not how much. The same full development charge is owed; it is simply due at occupancy instead of at building permit. The saving is the financing cost of not carrying that capital through the construction period, which can be significant on a multi-year build.

What is an occupancy permit for deferred development charges?

It is a Building Code mechanism, enabled under Bill 17, that lets a residential development be legally occupied while its development charges are paid on a deferred schedule rather than in full upfront. Without it, deferring the charge to occupancy would conflict with the normal requirement to pay before occupancy.

Does the development charge deferral apply to every project?

No. The deferral applies to eligible residential projects, and it is operationalized through regulation and municipal implementation. Before relying on it, confirm the project type is eligible, the enabling provisions are in force, and the municipality has implemented the deferral in its development charge by-law and permit process.

Can I combine the DC deferral with a development charge reduction?

Yes. Because the deferral does not change the amount owed, it stacks with the 2026 development charge reductions. A project can reduce the amount, where it qualifies as affordable or rental or benefits from the funded reduction, and defer the remaining balance to occupancy. The two are complementary levers.
