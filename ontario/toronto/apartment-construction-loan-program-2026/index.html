Toronto's $165M Apartment Construction Loan Program Announcement: October 2026

Published October 2026· Updated October 2026· 6 min read· PreBuildIQ Research Team
Quick Answer

On October 16, 2026, the federal government announced $165 million through the Apartment Construction Loan Program (ACLP) to support the construction of 370 rental homes in Toronto. The ACLP, formerly known as the Rental Construction Financing Initiative, provides low-interest repayable loans for the construction of new purpose-built rental housing. Loans are available for projects of five or more units and are structured to be repaid over a 10-year term after construction is complete. Projects must include a minimum share of units at or below median market rent to qualify. The October 2026 Toronto announcement is part of a series of ACLP commitments across Canada that the federal government has been making throughout 2026 as part of its housing supply strategy. For purpose-built rental developers in Toronto, the ACLP sits alongside the city's DC reduction program and the 2023 federal HST new rental rebate as a third cost-relief tool targeting the same segment.

Purpose-built rental in Toronto has been the subject of three major policy interventions since 2023: the federal HST rebate for new rental construction, the City of Toronto's development charge reduction program, and now a third $165 million commitment through the Apartment Construction Loan Program announced October 16, 2026. The latest announcement funds 370 new rental homes in the city through low-interest federal loans.

The ACLP is a loan program, not a subsidy. The federal government provides capital at below-market rates for construction, and the developer repays over a term that begins once the building is complete and leased. The interest-rate advantage is the value: in a market where construction financing is running at 7 to 8 percent, an ACLP loan at a materially lower rate changes the financing cost line on a rental pro forma meaningfully.

How the ACLP Stacks With Other 2026 Programs

For a Toronto rental developer, three cost-relief tools are currently live simultaneously:

  • Federal HST new rental rebate (2023). Removes the full HST from eligible new purpose-built rental projects, worth up to $130,000 per unit on a qualifying building. This is a direct cost reduction at construction.
  • Toronto DC reduction program (2026). Cuts Toronto city development charges 40 to 60 percent through 2029, with full DC elimination on 2+ bedroom rental units under the Mississauga-style rental DC elimination. The city DC saving is up to $36,140 per unit.
  • ACLP loan (October 2026). Provides below-market-rate construction financing, reducing the carrying cost on the construction loan through the build period. Value depends on loan size and the spread between the ACLP rate and the market rate.
These tools do not automatically stack

Each program has its own eligibility criteria, timing requirements, and application process. The HST rebate is accessed at tax filing, the DC reduction at building permit, and the ACLP requires a separate application and approval by CMHC before the loan is committed. Confirm eligibility for each independently.

Who Qualifies for the ACLP

ACLP loans are available to a broad range of rental housing developers, including private builders, non-profits, co-operatives, municipalities, and Indigenous housing organizations. Projects must be purpose-built rental, meaning the units will be rented rather than sold as condominiums. The affordability condition requires a minimum share of units priced at or below the area median market rent; the exact share is negotiated with CMHC as part of the application.

Projects with 50 or more units are prioritized, though smaller projects above the five-unit minimum can qualify. Construction must not have started before the loan is committed, which means developers need to apply before breaking ground, not after.

Example
A developer planning a 120-unit purpose-built rental building in Toronto's midtown ran three pro forma scenarios. Without any programs the project did not pencil at current construction costs and rents. With the HST rebate alone it was marginal. With the HST rebate plus Toronto's DC elimination on the 2+ bedroom units it became viable. Adding an ACLP loan, which cut the construction financing rate by roughly 2 percentage points on a 24-month build, moved the equity return above the developer's hurdle rate. The ACLP was the tool that turned "viable" into "approved for construction."

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the Apartment Construction Loan Program in Toronto?

The Apartment Construction Loan Program (ACLP) is a federal program administered by CMHC that provides low-interest repayable loans for the construction of new purpose-built rental housing. On October 16, 2026, the federal government announced $165 million through the ACLP to support 370 rental homes in Toronto.

How does the ACLP work?

ACLP provides below-market-rate construction loans for eligible purpose-built rental projects of 5 or more units. Loans are repaid over a 10-year term beginning after construction is complete and the building is occupied. Projects must include a share of units at or below area median market rent. Developers must apply before breaking ground, as the loan must be committed before construction starts.

Who can apply for ACLP in Toronto?

ACLP is available to private developers, non-profit housing organizations, co-operatives, municipalities, and Indigenous housing organizations building purpose-built rental housing in Toronto. Projects of 5 or more units are eligible, with priority given to projects of 50 or more units.

Can ACLP be combined with Toronto's development charge reduction?

Yes. The ACLP loan and Toronto's DC reduction program are separate and can be combined on the same project. The DC reduction applies at building permit, while the ACLP loan is committed through a separate CMHC application before construction starts. The 2023 federal HST new rental rebate can also apply to the same project, subject to each program's individual eligibility conditions.

How much is the ACLP announcement for Toronto in 2026?

The federal government announced $165 million through the Apartment Construction Loan Program for Toronto on October 16, 2026, to support the construction of 370 new purpose-built rental homes.
