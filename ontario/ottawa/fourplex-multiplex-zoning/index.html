Ottawa Fourplex and Multiplex Zoning 2026: What Is As-of-Right

Published August 2026· Updated August 2026· 7 min read· PreBuildIQ Research Team
Quick Answer

Under Zoning By-law 2026-50, Ottawa permits up to four dwelling units as-of-right on a serviced residential lot in the base neighbourhood zone, with no minimum parking requirement in urban and suburban areas. A fourplex that complies with the zone's height, setback, and lot standards proceeds directly to building permit with no rezoning or minor variance. Ontario's provincial baseline is three units as-of-right; Ottawa's fourth unit and zero parking minimums go further than most Ontario municipalities.

Ottawa is now one of the most permissive large cities in Canada for small multi-unit housing. The combination that matters for builders: four units as-of-right on a serviced lot, and no minimum parking requirement. Together those two rules remove the variance applications that used to accompany most infill multiplex projects, because unit count and parking were exactly where older zoning bit first.

As-of-right is a zoning statement, not a blank cheque. The fourplex still has to fit the neighbourhood zone's built-form standards, height, setbacks, lot coverage, and it still needs a building permit with full Ontario Building Code compliance. What disappears is the planning application: no rezoning, no Committee of Adjustment hearing, no public meeting for the unit count or the parking layout.

When You Still Need an Application

1
As-of-right

Up to four units, design compliant with all zone standards, serviced lot. Straight to building permit.

2
Minor variance

Unit count is fine but a numerical standard, height, setback, coverage, is exceeded. Committee of Adjustment, typically weeks.

3
Rezoning

More units or a bigger envelope than the neighbourhood zone permits, such as a mid-rise on a low-rise lot. Full zoning by-law amendment, typically a year or more.

One Ottawa-specific check before designing to the maximum: confirm the lot is fully serviced. The four-unit permission applies to serviced lots, and on partial services the unit count is more constrained. Rural and village lots run on different zones and standards entirely.

Appeal-status check

By-law 2026-50 is largely in force after the July 2026 OLT decision, but 25 appeals are outstanding. Before committing to a design, confirm the neighbourhood zone provisions for your specific lot are among the in-force portions.

Example
An Ottawa builder purchased a post-war bungalow on a serviced 15-metre lot intending a triplex conversion with a parking variance, the standard play under the old by-law. After 2026-50 came into force, the same lot supported a purpose-built fourplex with no parking stalls. The redesign added a fourth unit of rental income, deleted the variance timeline, and the project went to building permit in the same month it was redesigned.

Frequently Asked Questions

How many units can I build as-of-right in Ottawa?

Up to four dwelling units are permitted as-of-right on a serviced residential lot in the base neighbourhood zone under Zoning By-law 2026-50. The design must comply with the zone's height, setback, and lot standards. This exceeds Ontario's provincial baseline of three units as-of-right.

Do I need parking for a fourplex in Ottawa?

No. By-law 2026-50 eliminated minimum parking requirements for new residential development across urban and suburban Ottawa. You can build a fourplex with zero parking stalls if that fits your market, or add parking as a design choice. No variance is needed either way.

Do I need a rezoning or variance for an Ottawa fourplex?

Not if the design complies with the zone standards. A compliant fourplex on a serviced lot goes directly to building permit. A minor variance is only needed if a numerical standard like height or setback is exceeded, and a rezoning only if you want more than the zone's permitted form.

Does the four-unit rule apply everywhere in Ottawa?

It applies to serviced residential lots in the neighbourhood zones covering urban and suburban Ottawa. Lots on partial services, and rural or village lots, are governed by different zones with different unit permissions. Confirm your specific lot's zone before designing.

Is Ottawa more permissive than Toronto for multiplexes?

On parking, yes: Ottawa has no residential parking minimums in urban and suburban areas, while requirements vary elsewhere. On unit count the cities are comparable, four units as-of-right, though Toronto permits sixplexes in nine wards and Ottawa's neighbourhood zone structure scales to larger forms in higher-intensity zones. Both exceed Ontario's three-unit provincial baseline.
