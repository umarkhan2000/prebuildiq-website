Ottawa Zoning By-law 2026-50: What Is In Force and What It Permits

Published August 2026· Updated August 2026· 8 min read· PreBuildIQ Research Team
Quick Answer

Ottawa's new comprehensive Zoning By-law 2026-50 was approved by Council on January 28, 2026 and enacted March 11, 2026. Twenty-five appeals were filed, and on July 2, 2026 the Ontario Land Tribunal ordered that the portions not under appeal come into force retroactive to March 11, 2026, so the majority of the by-law now governs. Headline changes: up to four units as-of-right on serviced residential lots, minimum parking requirements eliminated across urban and suburban areas, and a new neighbourhood zone structure replacing the 2008 by-law's residential zones.

Ottawa just completed the largest zoning overhaul of any Canadian city this decade, and as of the Ontario Land Tribunal's July 2, 2026 decision, most of it is law. By-law 2026-50 replaces the 2008 comprehensive by-law with a new zone structure, permits up to four units as-of-right on serviced residential lots, and eliminates minimum parking requirements across the urban and suburban parts of the city. For anyone underwriting Ottawa land, the rules changed mid-project cycle, and the changes mostly cut in the developer's favour.

Status: What Is In Force

The by-law's path to force was staged, which is normal for a comprehensive by-law and worth understanding precisely:

The practical takeaway: the majority of By-law 2026-50 is in force today, but the 25 appeals mean specific provisions or geographies are still governed by the transition rules until the Tribunal disposes of each appeal. Before relying on a specific permission, confirm whether the provision that grants it is among the appealed portions. The City's zoning team and the OLT case file can both answer that question for a specific provision.

The Headline Changes

  • Four units as-of-right. A serviced residential lot in the base neighbourhood zone can hold up to four dwelling units without a rezoning or minor variance, subject to the zone's built-form standards.
  • Parking minimums eliminated. New residential and commercial development in urban and suburban zones no longer has a minimum parking requirement. Parking is a market decision, not a zoning one, and projects near transit no longer carry the cost of stalls the market does not want.
  • New neighbourhood zone structure. The old residential zone categories are replaced with a graduated series of neighbourhood zones that scale permitted intensity from low-rise to mid-rise as context supports.
  • One by-law city-wide. The comprehensive rewrite consolidates the patchwork of amendments accumulated since 2008 into a single instrument aligned with the current Official Plan.

What It Means for the Approval Path

The by-law moves a meaningful share of Ottawa's infill pipeline from discretionary approvals to permit-ready. A fourplex that complies with the neighbourhood zone standards goes to building permit, not to the Committee of Adjustment. A mid-rise that fits the new zone envelope avoids the site-specific rezoning it would have needed under the 2008 by-law. The applications that remain, larger rezonings, site plan control, still run through the standard Planning Act process, but the baseline the City measures them against is now the new by-law, not the old one.

Transition caution

Applications filed under the 2008 by-law, and provisions under appeal, are subject to transition rules. If your project straddles the changeover, confirm with City of Ottawa planning staff which by-law governs your file before you spend on design revisions.

Example
An infill builder holding a serviced corner lot in an inner-urban Ottawa neighbourhood had planned a triplex with two parking stalls under the old rules, with a variance expected for the parking layout. Under By-law 2026-50 the same lot supports a fourplex with no parking minimum. The builder redesigned to four units, dropped the variance, and went directly to building permit.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is Ottawa's new zoning by-law in force?

Mostly, yes. By-law 2026-50 was enacted March 11, 2026 and drew 25 appeals. On July 2, 2026 the Ontario Land Tribunal ordered that the portions not under appeal come into force retroactive to March 11, 2026. The majority of the by-law now governs, while appealed provisions remain subject to the appeal process.

Can I build a fourplex in Ottawa without a rezoning?

Yes, on a serviced residential lot in the base neighbourhood zone, up to four dwelling units are permitted as-of-right under By-law 2026-50. The building must still comply with the zone's height, setback, and lot standards, and it requires a building permit, but no rezoning or minor variance is needed for the unit count.

Does Ottawa still have minimum parking requirements?

No, not in urban and suburban areas. By-law 2026-50 eliminated minimum parking requirements for new residential and commercial development across nearly all urban and suburban zones. Developers decide how much parking to build based on the market rather than a zoning minimum.

What replaced Ottawa's old residential zones?

The 2008 by-law's residential zone categories were replaced with a graduated neighbourhood zone structure that scales permitted intensity by context, from low-rise neighbourhood zones permitting small multi-unit buildings to zones supporting mid-rise forms. Every parcel in the city was remapped, so a property's old zone code no longer describes its permissions.

What happens to the 25 appeals against By-law 2026-50?

The appealed portions remain before the Ontario Land Tribunal and do not come into force until each appeal is resolved, through settlement, withdrawal, or a Tribunal decision. If a permission you need sits in an appealed provision, the prior rules continue to apply to it in the interim. Check the OLT case status or ask City planning staff whether a specific provision is affected.
