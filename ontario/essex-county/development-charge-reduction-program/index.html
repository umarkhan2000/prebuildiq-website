Essex County Development Charge Cuts 2026: $105.8M Program, All Housing Types to December 2030

Published October 2026· Updated October 2026· 7 min read· PreBuildIQ Research Team
Quick Answer

On October 23, 2026, the Governments of Canada and Ontario announced up to $105.8 million for regional and local municipalities in Essex County through the Development Charge Reduction Program. In return, the Essex County municipalities are reducing or eliminating development charges for all housing types from March 30, 2026 to December 31, 2030. At four years and nine months, the Essex window is the longest of any Ontario DC reduction deal announced to date, giving Windsor-Essex builders more time to pull permits inside the savings window than anywhere else in the province. The funding covers Essex County along with its member municipalities and is part of the Canada-Ontario Partnership to Build.

Every Ontario DC reduction deal announced in 2026 has had a hard expiry date. Toronto and Mississauga both close March 31, 2029. Peel Region's 50 percent cut expired November 13, 2026. St. Thomas runs to March 31, 2029. Essex County is different: on October 23, 2026, Canada and Ontario committed up to $105.8 million for Essex County to reduce or eliminate development charges for all housing types through December 31, 2030, the longest window in the province.

The Essex County Market Context

Essex County includes Windsor and several surrounding municipalities: Amherstburg, Essex, Kingsville, Lakeshore, LaSalle, Leamington, Tecumseh, and the County itself. Windsor is the county seat and the dominant urban market. The region has historically offered lower land costs and development charges than the GTA, but the combination of industrial investment, US border proximity, and population growth from affordability-driven migration has increased housing demand.

For GTA-based developers looking at secondary markets, Essex County presents an unusual opportunity: lower baseline construction costs, the longest DC reduction window in Ontario, and a regulatory environment where the approval process is generally less complex than the multi-tiered GTA system.

Frequently Asked Questions

How are development charges changing in Essex County in 2026?

On October 23, 2026, Canada and Ontario announced up to $105.8 million for Essex County through the Development Charge Reduction Program. In return, Essex County and its member municipalities are reducing or eliminating development charges for all housing types from March 30, 2026 to December 31, 2030.

How long does the Essex County DC reduction last?

The Essex County DC reduction window runs from March 30, 2026 to December 31, 2030, approximately four years and nine months. This is the longest DC reduction window of any Ontario municipality announced under the Canada-Ontario Partnership to Build as of October 2026.

Does the Essex County DC reduction apply to Windsor?

Windsor is a member municipality of Essex County, so the commitment covers the Windsor-Essex region. However, because Essex County is a two-tier system, the specific charge reduction may vary by individual municipality. Confirm the applicable rate with Windsor or the relevant local municipality for your specific site.

Why is Essex County's DC window longer than Toronto or Mississauga?

The window is set by the terms of each individual federal-provincial deal. The Essex County agreement runs to December 31, 2030, which is approximately 21 months longer than the Toronto and Mississauga windows, which both close March 31, 2029. This likely reflects the size of the commitment, the housing target the governments are trying to unlock, and the longer timeline realistically needed to deliver that supply in the Essex County market.

What is the Canada-Ontario Partnership to Build?

The Canada-Ontario Partnership to Build is a framework under which the federal and provincial governments jointly fund municipalities to cut development charges. The federal-provincial funding backfills the DC reserve so the municipality can reduce charges without starving the capital funds needed to service new growth. Essex County is one of dozens of municipalities that have entered the program since its launch.
