GTA Development Approval Timelines 2026: Statutory vs Actual

Published August 2026· Updated August 2026· 7 min read· PreBuildIQ Research Team
Quick Answer

Development approvals in the Greater Toronto Area take far longer in practice than the Planning Act statutory clocks suggest. Site plan control has a 60-day statutory decision period but industry data shows GTA reviews averaging around 20 months, with residential nearer 16 months, mixed-use around 23 months, and non-residential up to 35 months. A zoning by-law amendment has a 90-day statutory clock (120 with an official plan amendment) but typically takes 12 to 24 months to council approval. Only minor variances resolve quickly, usually within 6 to 12 weeks.

The gap between what the Planning Act promises and what the GTA delivers is the central fact of Ontario development scheduling. The statute sets short decision clocks; the market runs on multiples of them. A developer who builds a pro forma on the statutory numbers is building on fiction. This page aggregates the realistic figures, by application type, so the schedule reflects reality.

Site Plan Approval: The 60-Day Fiction

Site plan control carries a 60-day statutory decision period once a complete application is accepted. Industry data tells a different story. Reporting on GTA and Ontario site plan review has shown average review times of roughly 20 months across all types, with residential projects nearer 16 months, mixed-use around 23 months, and non-residential developments as high as 35 months. Multiple review cycles, agency circulation, and completeness disputes account for most of the gap.

Rezoning: 90 Days on Paper, Two Years in Practice

A standalone zoning by-law amendment has a 90-day statutory clock before an applicant can appeal a non-decision; a concurrent official plan amendment extends that to 120 days. Neither reflects the real timeline. A typical GTA rezoning runs 12 to 24 months from first submission to council approval, and longer where an official plan amendment, regional co-approval, or an Ontario Land Tribunal appeal is involved. The statutory clock is a floor for appeal rights, not a forecast of approval.

Why the Gap Exists

Several structural factors stretch GTA approvals well beyond the statutory clocks:

  • Multiple review cycles: applications are rarely approved on the first submission.
  • Completeness disputes: the statutory clock only starts when an application is deemed complete.
  • Agency circulation: conservation authorities, regions, and utilities each review in parallel and can each return comments.
  • Study revisions: a single agency comment can require re-doing a technical study and re-circulating.
  • Council and committee scheduling: decisions wait for a meeting in the cycle.
What the delay costs

Carrying cost on a stalled GTA project runs about $43,000 per month. A single avoidable resubmission that adds three to four months is a six-figure loss, which is why getting the first submission complete is the highest-leverage decision in the schedule.

The One Fast Path: Minor Variance

Minor variances are the exception. Decided by the Committee of Adjustment rather than council, they typically resolve within 6 to 12 weeks, sometimes on the day of the hearing after the statutory notice period. This is why correctly identifying whether a proposal can proceed as a minor variance rather than a rezoning can compress a timeline from years to weeks.

Example
A mixed-use application budgeted its schedule on the 120-day statutory clock for a concurrent ZBA and official plan amendment. Agency circulation, two study revisions, and regional co-approval extended the actual timeline past 24 months. The carrying cost across the overrun exceeded a million dollars, none of which the statutory-clock schedule had anticipated.

Frequently Asked Questions

How long does site plan approval take in the GTA?

Site plan control has a 60-day statutory decision period under the Planning Act, but industry data shows GTA reviews averaging around 20 months. Residential projects average nearer 16 months, mixed-use around 23 months, and non-residential developments up to 35 months, driven by multiple review cycles and agency circulation.

How long does a rezoning take in Ontario?

A standalone zoning by-law amendment has a 90-day statutory clock (120 days with a concurrent official plan amendment), but a typical GTA rezoning takes 12 to 24 months from first submission to council approval. Applications involving regional co-approval or an Ontario Land Tribunal appeal take longer.

Why do development approvals take so long in the GTA?

Approvals stretch beyond the statutory clocks because of multiple review cycles, completeness disputes that delay the start of the clock, parallel agency circulation to conservation authorities and regions, study revisions triggered by agency comments, and council and committee scheduling. Few applications are approved on the first submission.

What is the fastest development approval in Ontario?

The minor variance is the fastest. Decided by the Committee of Adjustment rather than council, it typically resolves within 6 to 12 weeks, sometimes on the hearing day after the statutory notice period. Correctly using a minor variance instead of a rezoning can compress a timeline from years to weeks.

What is the difference between the statutory and actual approval timeline?

The statutory timeline is the legislated decision period under the Planning Act, such as 60 days for site plan or 90 days for a rezoning, after which an applicant can appeal a non-decision. The actual timeline is how long approval really takes, typically many months to years longer, because of review cycles, agency circulation, and scheduling.
